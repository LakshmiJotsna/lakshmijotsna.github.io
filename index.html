01 Myself

The mindset behind my engineering work.

I'm an Electronics and Communication engineer from India, now based in Rosenheim for my master's in Mechatronics. My work sits close to real hardware — controllers, PCBs, soldering, SMD assembly, communication interfaces, component decisions and BOMs — and systems that need reliability testing until they behave as intended.

I like practical engineering because it leaves very little room for vague thinking. A component either fits, a control signal either behaves, and a system either passes validation or teaches you what to fix next.

Engineering style

Datasheet discipline, validated on real boards.

Embedded thinking, careful component selection, hardware validation, and clean documentation that the next engineer can actually use.

Current direction

AI, robotics and machine automation.

Building systems that can imitate complex biological structures, adapt intelligently, and recreate parts of human problem-solving inside machines.

      What you're seeing

      • Every periodic signal is a sum of sine waves. Each rotating circle is one sine wave (a harmonic); stacking them tip-to-tail traces the orange curve.
      • More terms, better fit. Slide N up and the orange curve hugs the blue target more closely; the RMS error drops.
      • The Gibbs phenomenon. Near a sudden jump the sum always overshoots by about 9 %, however many terms you add. It only gets narrower.
      • Why it matters: the bar chart is the same signal in the frequency domain, which is what a spectrum analyzer (and my thesis) looks at.
